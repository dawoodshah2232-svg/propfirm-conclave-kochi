10 Questions to Ask
Before Choosing a Prop Firm

By the PropFirm Conclave team · Updated October 2026

Not all prop firms are equal — and the differences that matter rarely appear in ads. Before you pay an evaluation fee, run every firm through these ten questions.

Payouts & trust

  1. What is the profit split, and when do payouts happen? Look for a clear schedule (e.g. bi-weekly/monthly) and a stated minimum threshold — not vague promises.
  2. Is there public evidence of payouts? Independent trader reviews and payout proofs over time matter more than marketing.
  3. How long has the firm operated? New firms aren't automatically bad — but longevity is a meaningful trust signal in this industry.

Rules & fairness

  1. Exactly how does the drawdown work — static or trailing? Trailing drawdown is stricter than it looks. Get the precise definition in writing.
  2. What are the daily loss, consistency and news rules? The full rule list should be public before you pay — if it isn't, that's a red flag.
  3. What happens on rule breaches — warning or instant fail? Know whether one mistake ends the attempt.

Costs & scaling

  1. What's the total cost — evaluation fee plus any monthly/platform fees? Compare the all-in number, including any fees on the funded account.
  2. Is there a scaling plan, and what triggers it? A real scaling path (bigger capital for consistent traders) separates serious firms from fee collectors.
  3. Can I trade my style — scalping, news, EAs, weekends? Match the firm's allowed strategies to yours before committing.

Support

  1. How responsive is support, and in my timezone? Test it: email a question before buying. Slow pre-sales support predicts slow payout support.
If a firm can't answer these clearly before you pay, it won't answer clearly when your payout is delayed.

Red flags that should make you walk away

  • Rules that change after you purchase, or "interpretations" that appear only at payout time.
  • No verifiable company information or physical presence.
  • Guaranteed-profit marketing or pressure to buy "before the price doubles tonight."
  • Payout complaints clustering in recent independent reviews.
Risk disclaimer: This checklist is educational due diligence, not financial advice or an endorsement of any firm. Trading leveraged products carries substantial risk of loss.
