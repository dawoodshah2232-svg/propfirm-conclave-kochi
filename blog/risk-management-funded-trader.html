Risk Management Rules
Every Funded Trader Must Know

By the PropFirm Conclave team · Updated October 2026

Ask a funded trader what got them funded and you'll rarely hear about an indicator. You'll hear about risk: how much they risk per trade, when they stop for the day, and how they survived their worst week. Risk management isn't the boring part of funded trading — it is funded trading.

Rule 1: Risk a fixed fraction per trade

Most funded traders risk 0.5–1% of the account per trade. On a $100,000 account, that's $500–$1,000 of risk — not position size, risk: the distance to your stop-loss multiplied by your lot size.

Why so small? Because funded accounts die by drawdown rules, not by bad months. At 1% risk, you can lose ten trades in a row and still be down only ~10% — survivable. At 5% risk, four bad trades end your account.

Rule 2: Respect the daily loss limit like a circuit breaker

Set a personal daily stop well inside the firm's hard limit. If the firm allows 5% daily, stop yourself at 2–3%. The day you "need" one more trade to get back to breakeven is the day accounts die.

Professionals stop trading when their plan says stop. Amateurs stop when the account says stop.

Rule 3: Know your trailing drawdown at all times

On trailing-drawdown accounts, your "floor" rises as your balance peaks. Many traders blow accounts while technically in profit — because they forgot the floor moved up. Check it before every session; some traders keep it written on a sticky note on their monitor.

Rule 4: One position, one risk — no stacking

Opening three correlated positions (say EUR/USD, GBP/USD and gold, all dollar-weakness bets) is one big position wearing a disguise. Count correlated exposure as a single risk unit.

Rule 5: Journal every trade, review every week

Funded traders who last all do some version of this: log entry, exit, risk, setup type and emotional state — then review weekly for patterns. The journal is where "I keep overtrading Tuesdays" becomes visible and fixable.

Rule 6: Protect payouts, not just the account

Once funded, many traders withdraw a portion of early profits rather than compounding everything. Taking money off the table turns abstract numbers into real outcomes — and reduces the pressure that causes blowups.

The psychology underneath

Every rule above is really a rule about you: your impulse to revenge-trade, to size up after wins, to "just check" the charts at midnight. Systems beat willpower — build the rules into your routine so discipline isn't a daily decision.

Risk disclaimer: Trading leveraged products carries substantial risk of loss and isn't suitable for everyone. These are educational principles, not financial advice. Never risk money you can't afford to lose.
