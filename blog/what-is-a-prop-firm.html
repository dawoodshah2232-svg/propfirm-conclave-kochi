What Is a Prop Firm?
A Beginner's Guide to Funded Trading

By the PropFirm Conclave team · Updated October 2026

You've seen the phrase everywhere: "get funded." Trade with someone else's capital, keep most of the profit. It sounds almost too neat — so let's unpack what proprietary trading firms actually are, how the model works, and whether it makes sense for you.

The basic idea

A proprietary trading firm ("prop firm") is a company that puts up its own capital for traders to trade. Instead of risking your savings, you trade the firm's money. In exchange, the firm takes a share of the profits you generate.

The modern retail version works like this: you pay a fee to attempt an evaluation (often called a "challenge"). Pass it — by hitting a profit target without breaking the firm's risk rules — and you receive a funded account. Trade it well, and you keep a large share of the profits, commonly 70–90%.

Why do firms do this?

It's a talent-discovery business. Good traders are rare; capital is not. Rather than hiring traders as employees, firms run evaluations at scale: thousands attempt, a fraction pass, and the firm shares profits with the skilled minority. The evaluation fee covers the cost of running the program.

The firm provides the capital. You provide the skill. The risk rules protect both sides.

What "getting funded" actually means

  • Account sizes typically range from $5,000 to $200,000 in evaluation programs, with scaling plans that increase your capital as you stay consistent.
  • You don't own the capital — you trade it under the firm's rules. Break a hard rule (like the maximum daily loss) and the account is closed.
  • Payouts happen on a cycle — often bi-weekly or monthly — once you've earned above a threshold.
  • Costs are the evaluation fee plus, at some firms, a monthly platform or data fee on funded accounts.

Who is it for — and who should wait?

Funded trading suits traders who already have a tested strategy, disciplined risk habits, and several months of consistent demo results. It is not a shortcut for beginners: most evaluations are failed on risk-rule violations, not bad strategy.

If you're still learning, the honest path is: learn on demo, build a journal, prove consistency for 3–6 months — then consider an evaluation. (That's also exactly what Day 1 of the conclave is built for.)

The honest risks

Evaluation fees add up if you attempt repeatedly without fixing the underlying issues. Some firms have vague rules or slow payouts — due diligence matters enormously. And funded trading doesn't remove market risk: you can still have losing months; the firm's drawdown rules just cap how much.

Risk disclaimer: Trading foreign exchange, CFDs and other leveraged products carries a high level of risk and may not be suitable for all investors. Never trade with money you cannot afford to lose. This article is educational, not financial advice.
