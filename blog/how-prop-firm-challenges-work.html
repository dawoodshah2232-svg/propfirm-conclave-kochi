How Prop Firm Challenges Work:
Evaluations, Targets & Drawdowns

By the PropFirm Conclave team · Updated October 2026

The "challenge" is the gateway to funded trading — and the place where most attempts end. Understanding its mechanics before you pay the fee is the single highest-ROI thing a prospective funded trader can do.

The standard two-phase structure

Most firms run evaluations in phases on a demo account:

  • Phase 1 (the challenge): hit a profit target — commonly 8–10% — within a time window (often 30 days), without breaking risk rules. This is the filter.
  • Phase 2 (verification): a smaller target (commonly 4–5%) with a longer window. It exists to confirm Phase 1 wasn't luck.
  • Funded: pass both and you receive the funded account, usually with the same risk rules but real payouts.

Some firms offer single-phase ("instant funding") evaluations at higher fees — same idea, compressed.

The rules that actually fail people

Almost nobody fails a challenge because their strategy is bad. They fail because a rule they skimmed over ended the attempt.
  • Maximum daily loss — often 4–5% of the starting balance. One emotional revenge-trading session and you're done.
  • Maximum overall drawdown — often 8–12%. Some firms trail it behind your peak balance ("trailing drawdown"), which quietly tightens as you profit.
  • Consistency rules — caps on how much of your profit can come from a single day, to filter gamblers.
  • News & weekend rules — some firms restrict holding through major news or over weekends.
  • Minimum trading days — you may need to trade a minimum number of days, preventing one-lucky-trade passes.

What "trailing drawdown" means (with an example)

Say you start a $100,000 evaluation with a 10% trailing max drawdown. The line starts at $90,000. If you grow the account to $108,000, the line trails up to $98,000 — it locks in your progress. A drop to $97,999 ends the attempt, even though you're still "up" overall. Many traders learn this the expensive way; now you won't.

After you pass: the funded account

Funded accounts keep the same risk framework. Key differences: payouts become real (typically bi-weekly or monthly above a minimum threshold), some firms add a small monthly platform fee, and scaling plans can increase your capital after consistent profitable periods.

How to prepare properly

  1. Trade the exact rules on demo for at least a month — including the daily loss limit.
  2. Size positions so that 3–4 consecutive losses can't threaten the daily limit.
  3. Read the firm's full terms — especially trailing drawdown and news rules — before paying.
  4. Attempt when you're calm and routine-ready, not after a "hot streak" on demo.
Risk disclaimer: Trading leveraged products carries substantial risk of loss. Evaluation fees are non-refundable at most firms. This article is educational, not financial advice.
